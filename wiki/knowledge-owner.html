Knowledge Owner

The person accountable for a base, and the central idea of the playbook: knowledge needs a face. Agents take on most of the stewardship; the owner keeps what cannot be delegated.

Type ConceptOwner B. RousseauxStatus activeVersion 2.5Updated 2026-11-17
ownershipgovernance

A language model produces the most plausible answer; a company needs an answer someone stands behind. Knowledge needs a face. Embodiment is what makes governance real: a base can be governed because someone decides for it, and audited because someone answers for it. Without that person, rules apply to no one and an audit finds no one to question. The Knowledge Owner embodies the base: its scope, its choices and its limits.

Data management separates the owner, accountable for an asset, from the steward, who looks after it day to day. In a Knowledge Mesh, agents take on most of the stewardship: they draft pages, check sources, flag contradictions and assess freshness. The owner keeps what cannot be delegated:

Responsibility What it means
Orient Decide what the base is for, what it covers and what it does not
Arbitrate Settle contradictions and ambiguous cases, which agents escalate with a diagnosis
Commit Validate what the base says, and put their name on it
Listen Receive what consumers report, agents and people alike: drift, gaps, errors, unanswered questions
Retire Decide what is no longer valid

The owner does not reread everything: clear-cut changes go through, the owner handles the exceptions. Agents that consume a base are its most attentive readers; their signals flow back to the owner. One condition is not negotiable: the time an owner spends on a base must be recognised as part of their job. Otherwise the role becomes a chore, and the base slowly dies.
