The Knowledge Mesh Playbook

Knowledge is a pillar of AI at scale, alongside data. It must be managed as infrastructure, separately from the way it is consumed.

Type OverviewOwner B. RousseauxStatus activeVersion 2.5Updated 2026-11-17
foundations

If only the company knew what it knows. A playbook to bring the right knowledge, in the right shape, to the right place at the right time, for people and AI agents alike.

Knowledge management has thirty years of research, an international standard, dedicated roles and a market of tools. What is new is the reader: AI agents now consume a company's knowledge directly, and act on it at scale. What they do depends on the context they are given, and that context has to be steered and mastered, not left to chance. AI agents are systems built on AI models that pursue a goal, use tools and act on someone's behalf (AI agent). Yet organisations preparing for AI invest in data, models and platforms, and often treat knowledge as a side issue (Knowledge as a pillar of AI).

The playbook builds a bridge between what knowledge management already knows and what agentic AI now requires. Its organising principle is a separation, Principle zero: managing knowledge is one job, consuming it is another. Its operating model is the Knowledge Mesh, openly inspired by the Data Mesh: knowledge bases owned by domains, each with a charter and a Knowledge Owner who commits to what it says, federated by a few shared standards.

Key concepts at a glance

Concept Definition in the playbook
Management and consumption Two planes: knowledge is built, owned and governed in one, used by people and agents in the other
Knowledge base The unit of the mesh: a domain, a charter, an owner, sources, pages, a contract and states
Knowledge Owner The person who answers for a base: orients, arbitrates, commits and retires
Dynamic context Context assembled at the moment of use, from governed bases, within the user's rights, and traceable; the organisational side of context engineering
Knowledge Mesh A network of domain-owned knowledge bases, federated by shared standards

What it builds on, and what it adds

It builds on It adds
Thirty years of knowledge management research, methods and tools, which remain valid That knowledge has been underestimated as a success factor in deploying AI at scale
The tacit, human side of knowledge, which lives with people and communities That agents change who consumes knowledge, and therefore what "well managed" must mean
The Data Mesh, whose architecture it openly transposes An operating model for knowledge that fits agents, and that each organisation can adapt
Today's solutions, which already address many of the challenges described here The fundamentals that let an organisation choose, combine and replace them, in a world where people and agents share the same knowledge

How to read it

Sections 1 to 4 set the foundations: 1. The underestimated pillar, 2. Principle zero, 3. Where the Knowledge Mesh comes from, 4. Fundamentals we build on. Sections 5 to 8 describe the model, its challenges and its roles: 5. The Knowledge Mesh in brief, 6. The knowledge base, 7. The Knowledge Owner, 8. Dynamic context. The playbook borrows the rigour of an academic paper, sources and definitions, in plain language. It is written in its own words: sources mark the landmarks and inspirations behind each part, and the few direct quotations appear in quotation marks.
