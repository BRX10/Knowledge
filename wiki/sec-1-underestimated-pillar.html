1. The underestimated pillar

Knowledge management is a mature discipline, yet AI programmes overlooked it. Eight failure modes appear when agents meet the real corpus.

Type SectionOwner B. RousseauxStatus activeVersion 2.5Updated 2026-11-17
foundationsgovernance

Structure

  • A mature discipline
  • Why it was underestimated in the move to AI
  • Eight failure modes
  • What genuinely changes with AI agents

Key ideas

Key idea.

Agents raise the bar upstream. The effort moves from consuming knowledge to building it: owned, validated, dated and traceable before any agent reads it.

Concepts

  • Knowledge as a pillar of AI: To understand a situation, an agent needs data, what the company records, and knowledge, what it means and how to act on it. Knowledge deserves the same status as data.
  • Why knowledge was underestimated: Four reasons, offered as a practitioner's reading rather than a measured finding: the model seemed to know, retrieval was treated as engineering, governance followed data, and knowledge management was out of the room.
  • Eight failure modes: The same patterns recur when AI meets the real corpus. Each has a recognisable symptom, a specific cost for agents, and a principle of the playbook that answers it.
  • What agents change: The fundamentals of knowledge management stay. Agents change three things around them, the reader, the scale and the economics, and the effort moves to validation.

Sources & inspirations

  • Platt, attributed (1990s): the HP quote that frames the gap and the ambition
  • ISO 30401 (2018): shows that knowledge management is an established, standardised discipline
  • Nonaka (1994); Nonaka & Takeuchi (1995): the central insight that knowledge is created between people
  • McDermott (1999), why IT cannot deliver KM: the lesson of the first wave: tools alone do not deliver knowledge management
  • MIT NANDA (2025), The GenAI Divide: evidence of the gap between AI investment and measurable return
  • Dehghani (2019), monolithic data lake to data mesh: failure modes of centralised platforms, named one by one, as a model for the failure modes; the central bottleneck
  • Dehghani (2020), Data Mesh principles: technology advances that address volume but not the other dimensions of scale, behind the tool-first programme; governance that embraces several contexts rather than one canonical model
