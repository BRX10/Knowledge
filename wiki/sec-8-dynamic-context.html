8. Dynamic context

No delegation without grounding. Context assembled at the moment of use, from governed bases, within the rights of the person, and traceable.

Type SectionOwner B. RousseauxStatus activeVersion 2.5Updated 2026-11-17
contextconsumption-planeregulation

Structure

  • The need for grounding
  • From prompt to dynamic context

Key ideas

Key idea.

No delegation without grounding. Decisions and actions must be anchored in knowledge the company holds to be true, not in what a model finds plausible.

Definition.

Dynamic context is context assembled at the moment of use, from governed knowledge bases, within the rights of the person on whose behalf the agent acts.

Concepts

  • Grounding: Grounding is the shared basis on which joint action rests. Decisions and actions must be anchored in knowledge the company holds to be true, not in what a model finds plausible.
  • Context engineering: Since 2025, practitioners have moved from prompt engineering to context engineering, the discipline of choosing what goes into a model's working memory. The Knowledge Mesh adds its organisational side.
  • Dynamic context: Context assembled at the moment of use, from governed knowledge bases, within the rights of the person on whose behalf the agent acts. It is the consumption plane at its most demanding.
  • Traceability: Every element of an agent's context points back to a page, and every page to its sources and its owner, so that whatever an agent relied on can be inspected afterwards.

Sources & inspirations

  • EU AI Act (2024), Arts. 12, 14 and 26: traceability and human oversight required for high-risk systems, extended here as good practice for all agents
  • Clark & Brennan (1991), Grounding in Communication: grounding as the shared basis for joint action, applied to agents
  • Anthropic (2025), context engineering: the notion of context engineering, which dynamic context extends
  • Anthropic (2024), Model Context Protocol: the Model Context Protocol, as an example of open access standard
