# Distributed knowledge

The second foundation of the [[knowledge-mesh]] is thirty years of research and practice in knowledge management:

- Haridimos Tsoukas described the firm in 1996 as a distributed knowledge system that no one, not even management, can grasp as a whole.
- [[ikujiro-nonaka|Ikujiro Nonaka]] showed that knowledge is created close to practice, in the interplay between people, not in a central repository (see [[seci-cycle]]).
- Etienne Wenger located it in communities of practice.
- Richard McDermott explained in 1999 why the first wave of knowledge management tools disappointed: technology could store information, but knowledge depends on the people who use it and the communities that keep it alive.

This is the same diagnosis the [[data-mesh]] reached for data, from another direction. It leads to the playbook's stance, [[organised-fragmentation]].
