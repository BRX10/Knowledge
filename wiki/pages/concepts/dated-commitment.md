# Authority, not probability

Review does not guarantee truth, and the playbook says pretending otherwise would be dishonest. The owner's commitment is narrower and more useful: what the company holds to be true, as of a date, with someone answering for it. The statement is dated, sourced and open to challenge; it can be corrected, and the correction is traced. A probable answer, improvised at the moment of the question, offers none of that.

This is also what makes delegation possible: people entrust an agent with a task when they can see whose knowledge it acts on ([[delegation-levels]]).

> The owner turns a probability into a commitment. That is what an organisation can build on, and what an agent can be held to.

No owner, no authority: a base without an owner is just one more document, cleaner perhaps, but just as orphaned.
