# Data, information, knowledge

Thomas Davenport and Laurence Prusak described knowledge as "a fluid mix of framed experience, values, contextual information, and expert insight". That is why knowledge cannot be reduced to well-organised data.

The classic pyramid, which stacks data, information, knowledge and wisdom as successive refinements, is a useful picture, but it has been rightly criticised for that very shortcut ([[src-09-ackoff-fricke]]). Knowledge carries judgement, and judgement always belongs to someone, which is where the [[knowledge-owner]] comes in.

> Data tells an agent what the company records. Knowledge tells it what the company means.
