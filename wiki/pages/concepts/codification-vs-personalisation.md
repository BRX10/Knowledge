# Codification vs personalisation

In 1999, Morten Hansen, Nitin Nohria and Thomas Tierney distinguished two strategies: codification, which stores knowledge in documents, and personalisation, which connects the people who hold it. Most companies, they argued, should choose a dominant one.

> AI changes that last trade-off. When agents can draft, link and maintain pages, codification no longer has to come at the expense of the people who know: the expert stays in charge, the machine does the clerical work.

AI loosens another constraint. Organising knowledge has often meant heavy upfront work on classification and ontology before anything could be used. A base no longer has to wait for them: they can be built in parallel, with agents proposing structures and people validating them, so that the structure does not merely mirror the machine's own reading. Work starts on small perimeters (see [[start-small]]).
