# Knowledge as a self-describing product

The second principle transposes data as a product. A knowledge base serves two kinds of consumers, people and agents, and none exists without a charter.

The charter states the base's purpose, owner, audience and how it is built. It commits the owner, guides the agent that drafts the pages and tells other agents when to consult the base (see [[knowledge-base]]). The idea draws on Dehghani's data products that are discoverable, trustworthy and self-describing, with service level objectives, which also underpin the contract of a base. Part of the [[five-principles]].
