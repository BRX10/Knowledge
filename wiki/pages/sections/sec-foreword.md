# Why this playbook

The foreword starts from early retrieval-augmented generation projects: answers still missed the mark, and the reflex was to fix them with the system prompt. The real question was elsewhere: what does the AI actually know about the company, in other words what context does it work from? One of the first avenues was to turn documents into question-and-answer pairs, to make visible what the AI sees; the gap with what people believed they had written was often a surprise, and a lesson. It shifted attention from documents to knowledge. Four observations follow:

- **Trust.** As AI moves from answering to acting, delegating to an agent at scale requires understanding what it relies on: knowledge that can be read, owned and traced (see [[dated-commitment]], [[delegation-levels]]).
- **Organisation.** An organisational gap cannot be fixed by prompt engineering alone. A better prompt does not give a document an owner, settle a contradiction or retire an outdated rule (see [[knowledge-owner]]).
- **Pace.** Technology moves faster than any plan; staying agile means investing in what lasts: how an organisation knows what it knows, and who stands behind it (see [[portability]]).
- **Context.** Getting the best from AI is becoming a matter of giving agents the right context, and context is only as good as the data and knowledge it is drawn from (see [[context-engineering]]).

From these observations comes the intuition that knowledge deserves to be managed as a pillar of AI, alongside data ([[knowledge-as-a-pillar]]).

The ambition is to help align a shared vision: a common foundation, the smallest set of principles that organisations, teams and technologies can share, so that each can translate it into its own context, at its own pace (see [[open-questions]]).
